## Design Context

### Users

Compass serves developers, maintainers, and technical leads who need to understand dense or unfamiliar codebases without leaving their local workflow. The interface's primary job is to turn a large code graph into a fast, trustworthy navigation surface: orient at the architecture level, focus into a community or symbol, inspect exact relationship evidence, and open the corresponding source.

### Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and capable. Compass should feel like a professional code instrument rather than a marketing dashboard: technically dense without being noisy, confident without being decorative, and explicit about uncertainty, limits, and missing data.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a restrained workbench aesthetic that belongs naturally inside VS Code and remains equally coherent as a self-contained HTML export. In the extension, inherit VS Code light, dark, and high-contrast tokens; in exports, provide deliberate light and dark fallbacks. Prefer system/editor fonts, compact controls, crisp hierarchy, semantic graph color, and quiet surfaces. Avoid glassmorphism, ornamental gradients, oversized metric cards, excessive rounding, and motion without navigational purpose.

### Design Principles

1. **The graph is the primary surface.** Give the canvas maximum useful space and keep controls compact, contextual, and progressively disclosed.
2. **Density must remain legible.** Encode hierarchy, provenance, direction, and selection consistently; use filtering and focused views instead of shrinking everything into noise.
3. **Every interaction should preserve orientation.** Community drill-down, source navigation, zoom, search, and back actions must be predictable, reversible, keyboard-accessible, and visibly acknowledged.
4. **Evidence over implication.** Never invent graph meaning; distinguish aggregated, inferred, ambiguous, incomplete, loading, and error states in both copy and visuals.
5. **Performance is part of correctness.** Bound graph work, avoid avoidable re-layouts and duplicate transformations, keep initial rendering responsive, and degrade deliberately for large datasets.
6. **Honor the host and the user.** Follow VS Code theme and accessibility tokens, support light/dark/high-contrast and reduced motion, handle long paths and multilingual labels, and retain useful empty and failure states.
